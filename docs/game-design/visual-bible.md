# Game A — Visual Bible

Status: Official visual foundation  
Purpose: Define the production art language for Champions, environments, UI, materials, animation, lighting, VFX, and presentation.

---

## 1. Visual north star

Game A should look like a premium stylized 3D adventure designed for a living-room screen.

The visual goal is not to imitate a specific existing game or studio.

The target qualities are:
- animated-feature charm
- console-level readability
- strong silhouettes
- painterly color harmony
- expressive Champions
- clean environment composition
- restrained but satisfying VFX
- materials that feel crafted rather than flat
- visual richness without clutter

The visual style should remain achievable on affordable local HDMI-class hardware.

---

## 2. Style keywords

Premium stylized 3D.

Warm.

Readable.

Toyetic.

Painterly.

Expressive.

Adventure-forward.

Magical ancient world.

Soft forms with selective sharp accents.

---

## 3. Shape language

### Champions
Use one dominant silhouette concept per Champion.

Shapes should communicate personality before the player reads text.

Examples:
- round + low = sturdy/protective
- triangular + long = fast/precise
- wide + grounded = powerful
- tall + feathered = graceful
- segmented + articulated = clever/technical

Avoid overly realistic anatomy when it reduces readability.

### Environments
Use large shape masses first, detail second.

A strong scene should read in grayscale silhouette before texturing.

Foreground, midground, and background must be intentionally separated.

### Ancient structures
Architecture should feel constructed by the species/region that inhabits it.

Reptile ruins should not look like generic fantasy ruins.

Insectoid structures should not be stone castles with bug decoration.

Form must grow from region identity.

---

## 4. Champion design rules

Each Champion needs five layers of readability.

### Silhouette
The Champion should be identifiable from a black cutout.

### Face
Eyes/head orientation must read from gameplay camera distance.

### Regional DNA
The starter should clearly belong to its homeland.

### Ability DNA
At least one body feature should suggest its core gameplay role.

Examples:
- Mantari's blade arms
- Rumbli's rolling shell
- Aeri's wing profile
- Shelvora's defensive shell

### Evolution Canvas
The base model must leave visual room for future change.

Possible evolution surfaces:
- shell plates
- horns
- feathers
- fins
- markings
- crystals
- tail features
- limb armor
- membranes
- aura structures

Do not fully decorate the base starter. Growth needs somewhere to go.

---

## 5. Champion face language

Faces should be expressive without requiring film-level facial rigs.

Priorities:
- large readable eyes
- pupil direction
- eyelid/brow shaping
- head tilt
- neck motion
- blink timing
- mouth opening for reaction where useful

Emotion should come from the whole pose, not only facial controls.

Avoid human-like teeth and realistic animal aggression unless a specific Champion requires it.

---

## 6. Champion material language

Materials should be stylized-PBR, not flat color and not photoreal scan texture.

Desired material response:
- broad highlights
- readable roughness separation
- controlled surface variation
- strong base color
- selective clearcoat
- emissive detail reserved for magical progression

Examples:
- shell: satin/matte with limited clearcoat
- fur: broad soft response, not individual strand realism at first
- feathers: layered shape groups and roughness variation
- scales: large stylized clusters, not thousands of tiny scales
- crystal: saturated but controlled transmission/emissive feel

---

## 7. Evolution visual language

Evolution must preserve recognition.

Rule:
A late-game Champion should still visibly descend from its starter form.

Recommended progression hierarchy:

Stage 0 — starter form  
Stage 1 — markings/material changes  
Stage 2 — added secondary structures  
Stage 3 — silhouette modification  
Stage 4 — major evolution form

Relics should often begin with material/marking changes before adding large geometry.

This lets progression feel gradual and personal.

---

## 8. Regional visual system

Every region receives a defined kit:

- dominant natural form
- dominant material
- secondary magical material
- architecture family
- vegetation family
- relic shape language
- sky/atmosphere
- path language
- ambient motion
- Champion adaptation cues

Dominant forms:

- silk tension lines
- resin chambers
- hollow bark tubes
- segmented hive geometry

Palette:
- emerald
- deep moss
- cool teal
- dew blue

Materials:
- waxy leaf
- translucent dew
- fibrous bark
- silk

Lighting:
- shafts through canopy
- cool ambient shadow

Motion:

- spores

Avoid:

## 13. World map visual language

The world map should feel like a premium interactive illustrated diorama, not a flat menu.

Goals:
- readable continent silhouette
- region materials visible
- atmospheric motion
- region-specific landmarks
- subtle living details
- camera movement between highlighted regions

When selecting a region:
- camera eases toward it
- ambient sound changes
- color palette shifts
- one signature environmental animation plays
- selected starter silhouettes become discoverable

Locked/future regions should create curiosity rather than look disabled/gray.

---

## 14. Warden visual language

The Warden should visually contrast with the colorful regions.

Design:
- simple dark cloak silhouette
- soft cloth
- hidden or partially visible face
- one consistent ancient light source or emblem
- restrained movement
- minimal ornament

The Warden should feel old and intentional, not villain-coded through spikes/red eyes.

The Champion should always remain more visually appealing and colorful.

---

## 15. Shrine visual language

Resonance Shrines are recurring world anchors.

Shared visual grammar:
- circular or radial base
- region-specific vertical structures
- central suspended or embedded relic core
- dormant channels
- activation light traveling outward
- Champion-facing interaction space

Each region adapts the shrine:
- insectoid: silk/resin geometry
- beast: root/stone
- reptile: carved sandstone/jade
- winged: wind fins/sky crystal

Shrines must be readable from a distance.

---

## 16. Camera language

The default camera is cinematic but player-controllable.

Requirements:
- third-person follow
- orbit
- zoom range
- recenter
- smooth damping
- readable Champion framing
- slight run pullback
- no harsh vertical snapping

Default framing:
Champion occupies roughly 12–20% of screen height during standard traversal.

The environment must remain visible enough to support discovery.

During bond moments:
camera may temporarily take control for a short authored cinematic, then return smoothly.

---

## 17. Lighting rules

Lighting supports readability before realism.

General recipe:
- one dominant key light
- soft ambient/sky fill
- controlled shadow softness
- atmospheric depth
- selective local lights at points of interest

Do not light every object equally.

Use contrast to guide the eye.

Champion face and silhouette should remain readable during normal gameplay.

---

## 18. Color scripting

Each region should have:
- exploration palette
- mystery palette
- danger palette
- shrine/bond palette

Progression moments may temporarily shift the color balance to make emotional beats feel authored.

Example Sunscale:
exploration = warm sandstone + jade  
mystery = cooler shadow + jade glow  
bond = amber/gold + boosted warm light

---

## 19. Texture philosophy

Avoid procedural-noise-only textures as final art.

Production assets should use:
- hand-authored/painterly base color
- stylized roughness
- baked normal/AO where useful
- limited detail maps
- reusable trims or atlases for environment kits

Textures should support form, not hide weak modeling.

Hero Champions may receive more unique texture resolution than background props.

---

## 20. Environment composition rules

Every playable vista should have:
- clear foreground
- traversal middle ground
- destination/background landmark

Paths do not need glowing arrows if composition already guides the player.

Use:
- rock orientation
- plant clustering
- architecture
- light
- color
- height
- negative space

to create natural wayfinding.

---

## 21. Density rules

Premium does not mean filling every square meter.

Leave breathing room around:
- Champion
- paths
- interactive objects
- shrines
- camera

Detail density should increase near:
- destinations
- secrets
- story locations

Low-information traversal areas can remain simpler.

---

## 22. UI visual direction

The UI should feel native to the game world while staying extremely readable.

Style:
- dark translucent surfaces
- soft corners
- region-colored accent line/glow
- large typography
- minimal borders
- strong iconography
- animation with ease, not bounce overload

HUD should be sparse during exploration.

Persistent HUD candidates:
- small objective
- Champion identity
- optional ability indicator later

Menus may use:
- illustrated Champion portrait
- body/evolution silhouette
- relic slots
- skill path visualization

Avoid:
- dense RPG spreadsheets
- tiny item grids
- excessive permanent counters

---

## 23. Typography

Typography must read at TV distance.

Guidelines:
- strong bold display face for region/chapter names
- highly legible sans-serif for body/UI
- uppercase used selectively for labels
- generous line height
- avoid overly thin weights

No critical information should rely on 10px-equivalent text in living-room mode.

---

## 24. Animation principles

Animation should prioritize:
- clear anticipation
- grounded weight
- readable silhouettes
- follow-through
- personality

Every Champion needs:
- idle personality
- walk identity
- run identity
- turn weight
- jump/landing
- interaction reaction
- bond/evolution reaction

Animation should not be generic across body types.

A turtle-like Champion and a falcon-like Champion must not feel like the same rig with different meshes.

---

## 25. VFX principles

VFX are accents.

Use VFX to clarify:
- successful interaction
- ability state
- relic acquisition
- shrine activation
- region energy
- Champion evolution

Avoid constant particle noise.

The world should still look good with effects temporarily disabled.

---

## 26. Audio-visual coordination

Future sound design should reinforce visual identity.

Examples:
- jade crystal = soft glass/mineral hum
- silk = tension/whisper
- beast region = wood/grass/stone warmth
- High Aerie = wind tones
- Champion bond = region motif + Champion motif

Major visual changes should have matching audio punctuation.

---

## 27. Performance-aware art

Targeting affordable HDMI-class hardware means art discipline is part of the style.

Rules:
- strong geometry over excessive polygons
- reuse modular assets
- instance repeated props
- controlled transparent effects
- limited dynamic lights
- smart shadow budgets
- LOD where necessary
- texture atlases for environment families
- compressed GLB assets after visual approval

Quality should come from art direction and composition, not brute-force rendering cost.

---

## 28. Asset quality tiers

### Hero
Starter Champions, major evolved forms, Warden, major shrines.

Highest animation/material/detail attention.

### Featured
Region landmarks, important relics, story props.

Strong silhouette and unique materials.

### Modular
Rocks, plants, ruin pieces, path pieces.

Reusable and designed to combine without obvious repetition.

### Background
Distant mesas, canopy masses, clouds, skyline structures.

Low geometry; silhouette-first.

---

## 29. Production review checklist

Before approving a Champion:
- readable silhouette?
- clear face?
- grounded?
- regional identity?
- ability identity?
- room for evolution?
- attractive at gameplay distance?
- good in motion?

Before approving an environment:
- region recognizable?
- clear route?
- destination visible?
- foreground/midground/background?
- color hierarchy?
- enough breathing room?
- good from gameplay camera?
- performs within target?

Before approving UI:
- readable at TV distance?
- minimal?
- consistent accent language?
- does not cover important play space?

---

## 30. Vertical-slice visual target

The first polished benchmark is:

Sunscale Reach + Shelvora.

The slice should demonstrate:
- production-quality Champion silhouette
- believable locomotion
- region-specific environment art
- controlled camera
- polished lighting
- shrine focal composition
- visible relic transformation
- console-style UI presentation

Once this reaches the intended quality, it becomes the reference for all future regions and Champions.

---

## 31. North-star screenshot test

A still screenshot with no explanation should immediately communicate:

- this is a polished stylized adventure game
- that creature is the hero
- this is a specific magical region
- there is somewhere interesting to go
- the scene has intentional art direction

If a screenshot still reads as a tech demo, blockout, or generic asset scene, the visual pass is not finished.
